## Engineering Design Process
I am now advancing through the next stage of the Engineering Design Process [EDP], which involves researching existing technology in New York City's public transit system, then moving on to devising practical solutions to existing issues with existing knowledge. At this point in time, I have done my research on the existing technological advancements currently in use on the subway, and I have also planned a new potential solution that adapts to existing equipment while addressing one of the most persistent issues New Yorkers face daily in their commute. 

So far, I have dug deep into Communications-Based Train Control [CBTC]. This system is currently in use across multiple branches, such as Crosstown, Queens Blvd, Canarsie, and Flushing. The MTA has also recently released a plan hoping to fully extend the use of this new technology system wide by 2050. CBTC is a combined software-hardware based operating assistant aimed to reduce wait times and space trains closer together, while reducing the risk of collisions, derailments, and delays. The system was designed to replace 1930s-era signaling technology, which is rapidly approaching the obsolete phase and is now experiencing frequent failure, leading to delays and reroutes. The system works by equipping every train with on-board / internal technological components that allow the train to communicate back to the RCC [Rail Control Center] for precise location & speed measurements. This makes automated operation feasible, and runs dynamic braking zones & signal communication to allow trains to run closer together with a safety net, reducing wait times. This system also assists trains in immediate emergency braking to prevent collisions. This system is quite advanced and revolutionary for the subway system's complex infrastructure, which already dates back a century, yet most people do not realize its capabilities.

I had to perform deep research in order to reach past the general rundown and into the actual works of the system. I reached [official contractor diagrams](https://www.consystec.com/nycsra2022/web/spinstance.htm?id=PT01-02) detailing this infrastructure by Googling with actual official transit-related keywords, which led to me uncovering this diagram. This diagram explains how the system functions, and I was able to decode each box name to determine its role. I was then able to begin devising a potential draft for ACBTC [Advanced Communications-Based Train Control], a similar system that would rely on this technology to work around sudden service disruptions with instant RCC input without immediately having to resort to a full reroute or partial suspensions. This system would rely on using existing track infrastructure to automatically route affected trains away from the incident scene while minimizing service disruption, therefore eliminating the primary issue: delays.

I also looked into other pieces of technology in the system already visible to the human eye. I looked into the official design contractors for [subway help points](https://www.boycetechnologies.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/NYCTransit_HelpPoint_CaseStudy.pdf) and the new [countdown clock software](https://www.adamfishercox.com/portfolio/mta-platform-screens/) introduced in 2025, and based my new proposals for ACBTC on the general improvements to flaws in existing hardware. For instance, I noticed in the contracts that they primarily resolved capability and passenger accessibility. Older help points could not withstand weather challenges and the brutal conditions from both the environment and passengers in the subway, which led to Motorola introducing their new 7161 HP line, designed to eliminate these exact issues from public research. The same can be said for Adam Cox's countdown clock software, where he and his team researched existing software to weed out flaws, such as unnecessarily long route cycles, which they resolved and rolled out system-wide.

With these three topics, I decided to direct my primary focus onto resolving existing system issues, which led me to develop the ACBTC concept. I also have plans to research destination sign accessibility, in which reroutes will dynamically adjust overhead displays to reduce confusion by shifting away from a flat database.
